100 Thieves lifted the Stage 2 trophy in São Paulo on Saturday. By the time the draw show wrapped that same weekend, they already knew who's trying to end their Champions run before it starts.

## Group A Is Not a Reward

100 Thieves landed with T1, JD Gaming, and FUT Esports. The opening match is already locked: 100 Thieves vs T1 to open Group A play. I get why the seeding works this way, every region's top seed goes into a different group, so somebody was always getting the hard draw. But knowing the math doesn't make it less brutal to actually be 100 Thieves right now. T1 is the kind of Pacific team that shows up to international events playing better than their own league suggested all split. JD Gaming is China's number one seed, in Shanghai, with a home crowd that's going to be deafening every time they touch a site.

## NRG Isn't Getting a Break Either

The defending champions dropped into Group D against Nongshim RedForce, fresh off winning Masters Santiago, with Karmine Corp and Xi Lai Gaming rounding out the group. Nongshim has been the story of the back half of this year. Holding the trophy from last year means nothing to a team that just won the last international event it entered.

## Where I Land

Winning Stage 2 was supposed to buy 100 Thieves a soft landing into groups. Instead they open against a Pacific team built for exactly this kind of stage, in the one city where JD Gaming gets to play at home. The trophy from São Paulo is real. So is Group A. Champions starts on the 24th, and for two of my favorite teams in this field, it starts with the hardest version of the bracket possible.

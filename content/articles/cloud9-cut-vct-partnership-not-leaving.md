Cloud9 losing its VCT spot is a bigger deal than the format news, and their reaction is the best part of it.

## Who Got Cut
Riot dropped every region from ten partners to eight for 2027 and 2028. In Americas that took out Cloud9, FlyQuest, Shopify Rebellion and M80. Some lists also have ENVY and LYON out, and outlets don't agree yet, so I'm not going to pretend the full list is settled.

The M80 fallout hit first. Kyu announced his time there is ending, and CEO Marco Mereu confirmed the org wasn't offered a spot after six years in Valorant. He never said whether M80 would play the open circuit.

## Two Ways To React
Ludwig said Shopify Rebellion should abandon Valorant, then walked it back and argued that unpartnered orgs can't build a title roster because partnered teams will poach the players. Is he right? Partly. Poaching is real. But "we can't win" is a strange thing to say before a single open qualifier is played.

Cloud9 went the other direction. Their post said they remain committed to VCT and, in their words, "We are not leaving." They've been running open tryouts in 10-player lobbies to build a roster for the open qualifiers.

## My Take
I'd rather watch a cut org grind its way back than a partnered one coast. Kickoff in January has twelve teams per region, eight partners and four qualifiers. Four seats is not many, and Cloud9 has to fight for one.

I could be wrong about the tryouts. Open lobbies don't guarantee a roster that can beat KRU or MIBR over a best of three. But it's the only plan that doesn't involve waiting for Riot.

The open qualifiers start in November. That's when we find out who meant it.

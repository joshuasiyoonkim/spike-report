Leviatán won Masters London in June. In September, they're staying home for Champions.

## The Streak That Had to Break Eventually

Every Masters winner since the format started in 2021 went on to play Champions the same year. Leviatán is the first team to break that streak, and they broke it by losing exactly twice in the two weeks that mattered most. They dropped the Stage 2 upper semifinal to 100 Thieves 2-0 on August 28, Lotus 13-2 and Summit 13-7, then lost the elimination match to G2 two days later, 2-1, closing on a 13-8 Summit that sent them home instead of to Shanghai.

## Championship Points Don't Care About June

I get why this feels wrong. Leviatán lifted a trophy in London against a stacked field, and four months later that trophy buys them nothing at Champions. But the points system weighs Stage 2 heavier than every other stretch of the calendar, on purpose, so a team can't coast into Shanghai on one great week in the spring. Leviatán's Stage 2 was a fine group stage and a playoff run that ended in back to back losses. The math didn't care what happened in London.

## Where I Land

I don't think the system is broken. I think Leviatán is. A roster good enough to beat the entire world in June should not lose two winnable series to close out the year, and blaming the points format is easier than admitting that. G2 earned the spot by going through Leviatán to get it. Leviatán will watch Shanghai from home, and the trophy from London won't be much company.

Four years is the right number. Riot banned all five BONK players and their head coach for 48 months today, and nobody should be asking for less.

## What actually happened?

On November 6, BONK beat Derke's Aurora 2-1 in the Round of 32 of EMEA Kickoff Open Qualifier #1, including a Bind they took back after trailing 3-11. They kept winning and made the top four. Two days later Riot disqualified them, and today's ruling filled in the rest: cheating software was running on a computer tied to the head coach, feeding live game info like enemy positions, and all five players knowingly took part.

## Why the coach part matters

Is this a normal cheating case? On paper, sure. In practice, no. Everyone's mental model of cheating is one guy with a wall on his own PC. Vanguard sits on the player's machine. This was the info living somewhere else and getting passed along.

That's the version I worry about. I've had comp games where I was sure someone knew exactly where I was every round, and I always assumed it was the person in the lobby. It never crossed my mind that the help could come from outside the game entirely.

## Was it too harsh?

The other reading is that players might have been pressured by their coach, and four years for the whole roster ends careers that might have been salvageable. I get it. I just don't buy it here, because Riot found every player knowingly participated. You can't sit on that comeback against a Masters champion and claim you didn't notice.

Aurora got their shot back, and open qualifiers just got a little more paranoid. Good. Check the coach's screen too.

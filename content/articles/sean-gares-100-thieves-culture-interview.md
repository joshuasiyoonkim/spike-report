100 Thieves are two weeks from walking into Group A against T1 with a trophy already in the case. I've spent all year writing about the results. Sean Gares' new interview with Hotspawn is the first time anyone's actually explained the machine behind them.

## The Vora Story Is the Whole Article

Nbs found Vora as a rank one flex Initiator at TSM and decided to turn him into an IGL from scratch. Not slot him in as a caller because nobody else wanted the job. Build him into one. Gares said he grilled Vora in that interview "for a long time" and Vora "just kept answering at an insane rate." That's not a guy who got the armband by default. That's a young player who earned it under direct pressure before he ever touched a Champions stage. I've watched enough amateur teams implode because the best mechanical player got handed shotcalling duties nobody prepared them for. 100T did the opposite on purpose.

## Gares Learned From His Own Bad Experience

The detail that stuck with me: Gares said an assistant coach was once pushed onto him with no say in it, and he refused to do that to Nbs. He let Nbs pick d00mbr0s himself, then split the lane clean, Nbs runs macro, d00mbr0s handles the individual conversations. That's not a small thing for an org that's had its share of public roster dysfunction. Somebody in that building got burned once and changed the process instead of just hiring smarter next time.

## Where I Land

Stage 2 champions, EWC champions, first seed at Champions. None of that reads like an accident anymore. It reads like a front office that fixed the process first and let the results follow. T1 in Group A is still the hardest draw in the field. But now I know why I'm not worried about 100T showing up to it unprepared.

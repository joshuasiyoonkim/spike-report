LOUD had this one won twice before they actually won it.

## Two Different Ways to Blow a Lead

Saturday's Lower Final opened with LOUD taking Abyss and Sunset to go up 2-0, the kind of start that should end in a rest day before the Grand Final. NRG had other plans. They clawed back Summit, then buried LOUD 13-5 on Haven to force a fifth map with both seasons on the line. I've watched enough lower-bracket deciders to know how often a 2-0 lead like that turns into a story about the collapse instead of the win. It didn't this time.

## Lotus Wasn't Close

LOUD closed it out 13-3 on Lotus, the exact margin 100 Thieves used to run over NRG on the same map two days earlier. tkzin's Neon set the tone early, and NRG never found the round that makes a decider feel dangerous. However LOUD got here, through the lower bracket, past G2, past nearly blowing that 2-0 lead against a team that's beaten them before, they're the ones playing Sunday.

## The Stakes Are Smaller Than They Look

Here's the strange part: nobody's Champions spot actually needed this result. NRG were Shanghai-bound anyway. 100 Thieves clinched their spot sweeping NRG in Friday's Upper Final. LOUD punched their own ticket just by reaching this final, their first Champions appearance since 2023. Sunday's Grand Final against 100 Thieves is for the Stage 2 trophy and seeding, not survival, and somehow that makes it more interesting, not less.

## Where I Land

I had 100 Thieves handling this bracket wire to wire, and they still might. But a team that watches its own 2-0 lead disappear and still closes with a 13-3 isn't who I want to face in a best of five. LOUD in four.
